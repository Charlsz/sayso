# Voice

Read this when the output should sound like a particular person, especially in a personal message or a social post.

The job is that this recipient can answer it as a message from this person. If a sentence already does that, keep it. Change the word that blocks the meaning, and leave the rest of the sentence.

- Keep their capitalization, sentence length, slang, humor, and directness when those are part of the draft or of a preference they stated.
- Change a word when it hides the meaning, drops something they meant to include, or creates a problem they asked you to fix. Leave a characteristic sentence alone.
- Do not replace a vague phrase with a place, event, or motive you guessed.
- A text, a caption, and a letter have different shapes. Match the one they are writing.
- Do not add hashtags, emoji, greetings, or sign-offs they did not use, unless the format requires a sign-off they already supplied.
- Do not sand off bluntness, warmth, or humor to make the message more proper, unless they asked for that change.
- Do not invent a closer relationship, or a colder one, than the draft shows.
- If the draft is already clear for that recipient, return it as they wrote it.
- These habits belong to this piece. Do not treat them as the way every later task should sound.
