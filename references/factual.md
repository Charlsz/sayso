# Factual writing

Read this for news briefs, research summaries, literature notes, and edits that must stay faithful to sources.

The standard is that a careful reader can see what was measured, what was claimed, and what is unknown.

- Use the sources given. Do not fill gaps with outside knowledge, round a figure into a cleaner one, or upgrade a claim because it sounds plausible.
- Keep a figure attached to the method that produced it. Two measurements of the same idea are still two results. A survey and an experiment are different designs.
- Attribute a statement to the person or document that made it. A quote is not a finding. An unconfirmed account stays unconfirmed.
- Preserve definitions, sample sizes, populations, settings, and stated limits. Those are part of the result.
- If the sources conflict or stop short, say so. Do not close the gap by choosing the smoother story.
- Keep a result the source establishes separate from a tendency, a condition, or an open question. Do not promote one of those into another.
- Keep the register of the requested format. A journal abstract stays formal. A newsroom note stays plain. Neither becomes a casual explainer.
- Do not add causes, recommendations, or implications the sources do not support.
